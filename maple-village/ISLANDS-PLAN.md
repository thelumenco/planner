# Islands by boat and ferry: the plan (for approval, nothing built yet)

Three new destinations, built one at a time to the destinations playbook (HANDOFF.md): four outdoor screens in a
2×2 grid, at least five interiors, their own art, music, locals with stories, a souvenir collection, local goods
that feed the kitchen, gelato and bonbons, and new things for your 5-heart friends to do. Day trips only, home the
same night. Jeju is already done.

Every island has a ferry from the foreshore jetty, like Jeju. Every island has four outdoor screens and at least five
interiors, with something to take part in in two or three of them. Your friends come along instead of family.

Suggested order: **Saltwick Harbour first** (closest, and it gives the boat a job too), then **Hokkaido**, then **Bali**.
Each one is a big round on its own.

## How you get there

| Where | How | Cost | Who comes | Hours |

The crossing gets its own little ride scene (like the train ride): gulls and a lighthouse for Saltwick, ice floes
and a crane flying over in winter for Hokkaido, flying fish and a volcano on the horizon for Bali.

## 1. Saltwick Harbour (the ferry, or your own boat)
**Inspiration:** the North Yorkshire fishing villages: Robin Hood's Bay, Staithes, Whitby. Steep, crooked, salty
and cosy.

**Geography:** a narrow cove between two cliffs. Red pantile roofs stacked up a steep slope, cobbled lanes ("wynds")
zig-zagging down to the water, a harbour wall curling round like an arm with the lighthouse at its tip. Grey-green
sea, grey stone, rust-red roofs, the light from the east in the mornings.

**Layout (arrive bottom right, at the harbour):**
```
  sw_clifftop   | sw_wynds
  --------------+---------------
  sw_beach      | sw_harbour  (you moor here)
```
- **Harbour:** the fish market, the chandlery, the harbour wall and lighthouse, your boat.
- **Wynds:** the steep lanes, the quay café, the smokehouse, the little shops.
- **Beach:** the scar (flat rocks at low tide), fossil hunting, rock pools, a picnic spot on the slipway.
- **Clifftop:** the coastguard cottages, the abbey ruin on the headland, sheep, the cliff path, a bench with the view.

**Interiors (5):** the fish market hall, the chandlery, the quay café, the smokehouse, the lighthouse (climb to the
lamp room).

**Flora and fauna:** sea pinks (thrift) in spring, heather on the clifftop in late summer, gorse all year;
kittiwakes, herring gulls, puffins offshore in summer, seals on the scar, a harbour cat.

**Local goods (Mel's picks):**

|---|---|---|
| Lobster | 18 | a new tapas (lobster with garlic butter) |
| Clams | 8 | a new tapas (clams in white wine) |

**Things to take part in:** fossil hunting on the scar (tap the rocks, sometimes an ammonite), crabbing off the harbour
wall with a line and bacon, lighting the lighthouse lamp at dusk with the keeper.

**Locals (2 or 3, with short story arcs):** Ruth the fishmonger, last of four generations at the market; Alfie the
lighthouse keeper, who logs every ship in a ledger; Nell at the quay café, who came for a weekend and never left.

**Souvenirs:** ammonites (six kinds). The full set goes in a glass case in your house.

**Friends' new acts:** crabbing, fossil tapping, skimming stones, eating chips on the harbour wall (guarding them from
the gulls).

**Puffin Isle (Mel's earlier idea):** could be a short extra hop from Saltwick by your boat (one screen: the
lighthouse keeper, rock pools, seabirds, sea glass). Question below.

## 2. Hokkaido (the ferry)
**Inspiration (Mel's picks):** four corners of Hokkaido, one per screen: Otaru's canal and glassworks, Sapporo with its
beer factory, a Furano flower farm, and Niseko under Mount Yotei. In real life they're hours apart; here they sit side by
side, the way Jeju's screens do. Snowy in winter, lavender in summer.

**Geography:** a cold northern coast at the top (Otaru's harbour faces the sea), the city in the north-east, the
mountains and ski slopes in the south-west, and wide rolling farmland in the south-east. Pale cool colours: grey-blue
stone, white, lavender, birch-bark and soft green. It must not feel like Kyoto: no temples or maples, it's wide, open
and northern.

**Layout (arrive top left, at Otaru's ferry pier):**
```
  hk_otaru     | hk_sapporo
  -------------+---------------
  hk_niseko    | hk_furano
```
- **Otaru:** the ferry pier, the canal with its stone warehouses and gas lamps, the glassworks, the music box hall, a
  little fish market on the quay (crab, scallops).
- **Sapporo:** the red-brick beer factory with its tall chimney, the white clock tower, a ramen alley, the long park
  down the middle of the city (snow sculptures in February, a beer garden in summer).
- **Niseko:** Mount Yotei on the skyline, the ski slopes and lift (snow December to March; green hiking slopes and the
  river in summer), the ski lodge, a hot spring foot bath in the village.
- **Furano:** the flower farm (lavender in July, striped rainbow fields all summer), the dairy and cheese house, melon
  stalls, the lone tree on the hill.

**Interiors (5):**
- **Otaru glassworks:** furnaces, shelves of glass floats and lamps.
- **Otaru music box hall:** walls of music boxes, a big steam clock outside the door.
- **Sapporo beer factory:** copper brewing kettles, a tour along the brewing line, the long tasting hall.
- **Sapporo ramen shop:** a counter with stools, steam, miso ramen.
- **Niseko ski lodge:** ski hire, a fire, hot chocolate, boots drying by the stove.

(A sixth, the Furano dairy and cheese house, if you'd like one.)

**Things to take part in:**
- **Otaru glassworks:** blow a glass float and pick its colour.
- **Otaru music box hall:** choose a tune for a music box.
- **Sapporo beer factory:** the brewing tour, then a tasting where you match three beers to their notes (like the
  olive oil tasting).
- **Niseko:** a ski lesson in winter (three gentle runs down the nursery slope), river rafting in summer, and the
  hot spring foot bath all year.
- **Sapporo, February:** help build a snow sculpture in the park.

**Seasons:** deep snow from December to March (snow sculptures in Sapporo and snow lanterns along Otaru's canal in
February), cherry blossom late, in May, lavender in July, gold fields in autumn.

**Flora and fauna:** lavender, poppies, birch trees, potato flowers; red-crowned cranes (winter), Ezo red foxes, Ezo
deer, a little owl.

**Local goods:**
| Good | Price | Use back home |
|---|---|---|
| Hokkaido milk | 6 | gelato base, bonbons (a milk chocolate) |
| Furano melon | 20 | gelato, a gift |
| Scallops | 12 | a new tapas |
| Sweetcorn | 3 | a buttered corn tapas, corn gelato |
| Sapporo beer | 6 | a gift; beer-battered fish at home |

**Locals:** Haruki the glassblower in Otaru (third generation), Kenji the brewer in Sapporo, who's worked the kettles for
forty years, and Aiko the ski instructor in Niseko, who grew up on the slopes under Mount Yotei.

**Souvenirs:** music boxes, one for each season's tune (four). Complete the set and they play in your house.

**Friends' new acts:** foot bath, soft-serve, snowball fights (winter), lying in the flower fields, a beer garden toast
(all checked against every earlier town so nothing repeats).

## 3. Bali (the ferry)
**Inspiration:** Ubud and the rice terraces of central Bali, with a little beach. Green, warm, gentle, with
offerings everywhere.

**Geography:** steep green terraces stepping down a valley, a river gorge with jungle, a village of carved stone
gateways and thatched pavilions, and a black-sand beach with outrigger boats. A volcano on the skyline. Warm,
deep greens, black stone, gold, frangipani white.

**Layout (arrive bottom left, at the beach):**
```
  bl_terraces   | bl_temple
  --------------+---------------
  bl_beach      | bl_village
```
- **Beach:** the ferry jetty, the outrigger boats, a warung (little café), a sunset spot.
- **Village:** the market, the silversmith, the palace courtyard where the dancers practise.
- **Terraces:** the rice terraces and their water channels, a swing, a coffee and cacao farm.
- **Temple:** the split gate, the water temple, the monkey forest (they steal snacks).

**Interiors (5):** the market, the silversmith, the warung, a cacao and coffee house, the dance pavilion.

**Flora and fauna:** rice, frangipani, hibiscus, banana and coconut palms, bamboo; long-tailed macaques, geckos,
ducks herded through the paddies, kingfishers.

**Local goods:**
| Good | Price | Use back home |
|---|---|---|
| Balinese cacao | 9 | bonbons through discovery |
| Vanilla pods | 10 | gelato, bonbons, baking |
| Coconut | 4 | gelato (coconut), a coconut tapas |
| Snake fruit (salak) | 5 | gelato, a gift |
| Palm sugar | 4 | gelato and bonbons (a caramel) |
| Lemongrass | 3 | infused oil at the mill, a tea |

**Things to take part in:** making a canang sari offering (a little palm-leaf tray of flowers), a gamelan lesson
(tap the rhythm), watching the dancers practise and learning one hand movement, making a silver ring.

**Locals:** Wayan the silversmith, Ketut the rice farmer who knows every water channel, Made at the warung.

**Souvenirs:** carved wooden animals (six), set out on a shelf at home when complete.

**Friends' new acts:** herding ducks, the jungle swing, a coconut on the beach, feeding a (well-behaved) monkey.

## Balance
- Goods follow the usual rule: sell prices never above buy prices.
- What you make from them earns a little more than the goods cost, as everywhere, but the fare makes each trip a treat
  rather than a money-maker.
- Saltwick is cheap to reach once you own the boat; Hokkaido and Bali cost more the further they are.
